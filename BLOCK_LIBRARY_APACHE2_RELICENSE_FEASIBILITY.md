# Block Library Apache-2.0 Relicense Feasibility

Assessment date: 2026-08-29
Decision: **do not relicense in this candidate**
Immediate candidate license: **existing MIT License**

## Executive assessment

The Block Library can proceed as an MIT-licensed candidate without changing the repository's current license. A full repository-wide relicense to Apache License 2.0 is not presently supported by a demonstrated complete contributor and rights chain. The six-record publication-authority attestation closes the identified UMG project-authority gap for those records, but it is narrower than the evidence needed to relicense every copyrightable contribution in the repository.

## What a full Apache-2.0 relicense would require

Before changing the repository license, the project should establish and preserve a complete rights analysis that covers, at minimum:

1. an inventory of copyrightable files and material in the proposed relicensing scope;
2. the contributor or rights holder for each material contribution, including imported or derived material;
3. the license and provenance under which each contribution entered the repository;
4. documented permission from every necessary rights holder to distribute the covered contribution under Apache-2.0, or a documented basis showing that such permission is unnecessary;
5. treatment of third-party dependencies, fixtures, examples, schemas, generated outputs, trademarks, and material whose license is not controlled by UMG;
6. retention of notices and attribution obligations required by existing licenses;
7. legal review where ownership, employment, contractor, assignment, or derivative-work status is uncertain; and
8. a reviewable decision record mapping the evidence to the exact commit and files being relicensed.

Project-owner authority over UMG-originated material may satisfy part of that analysis, but it cannot substitute for permissions held by other contributors or third parties. A human project-authority attestation is also not independently recovered drafting history.

## Candidate decision

- Preserve the existing `LICENSE` file and MIT terms unchanged.
- Publish the immediate candidate, if all other qualification gates pass, as MIT-licensed material.
- Do not describe this candidate as Apache-2.0, dual-licensed, or approved for an Apache-2.0 transition.
- Treat an Apache-2.0 relicense as a separate future governance and rights-clearance project.

## Revisit gate

A future Apache-2.0 proposal is feasible only after the complete contributor/rights chain is assembled, exceptions are resolved or excluded, required permissions are recorded, notice obligations are prepared, and the resulting exact-tree proposal receives formal approval. Until then, MIT remains the operative license for the immediate candidate.
